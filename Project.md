# Project Overview

This final project requires you to analyze how a platform company from **your home country** has used platform strategies over the past five years (2021–2025), applying the platform strategies and rules you learned in this course. The company may be listed on a stock exchange or privately held.

## Learning Objectives

By completing this project, you will:

- Apply the platform strategies and their rules from this course to a real company
- Use performance evidence (financial statements or public performance indicators) to judge whether a platform strategy worked
- Evaluate strategic shifts and their market impact
- Develop professional business reporting and presentation skills

## Company Selection

**Select ONE company that meets the following criteria:**

1. **Home country**: The company was founded in, or has its headquarters in, **your home country**
2. **Listing status does not matter**: Both listed and privately held (unlisted) companies are allowed
3. **Platform business**: The company operates a platform, which means it connects two or more groups of users (for example, buyers and sellers, riders and drivers, or users and advertisers)
4. **Evidence available**: Public sources show how the company's strategy changed over the **past 5 years (2021–2025)**, and you can find at least **3 performance indicators** reported by year (see *Performance Evidence* below)

### Suggested Company Types

- Technology platforms (e.g., e-commerce, social media, sharing economy)
- Financial technology (fintech) platforms
- Traditional companies transitioning to platform models
- Marketplace operators
- Digital service providers

## Performance Evidence

**Performance evidence** is any number that shows whether the company's strategy worked. Many privately held companies do not publish financial statements, so you may use either type of evidence below.

**Option A – Financial statements** (if available)

- Annual reports or audited financial statements
- Revenue growth, revenue mix by business line, operating profit, and take rate (the share of each transaction the platform keeps)

**Option B – Public performance indicators** (if financial statements are not available)

Present **at least 3** of the following indicators, reported **by year**:

- Number of users, sellers, drivers, or other participants on each side of the platform
- Gross merchandise value (GMV), which is the total value of transactions on the platform
- App downloads or app store rankings
- Funding raised and company valuation from investment rounds
- Market share from industry reports or credible news media
- Figures published in the company's own press releases or official blog

- **Privately held companies**: some countries require certain unlisted companies to publish audited statements in a public registry; check your country's rules first. Otherwise, use the company's press releases, app statistics sites, investment news, and industry reports.

# Analysis Framework

Your analysis has three parts. **Part A** describes the platform with the ideas from Module 1. **Part B** judges 2–3 strategies from Modules 2 and 3 with the rules you learned in class. **Part C** recommends what the company should do next.

## Part A: Platform Diagnosis (Module 1)

Describe the platform before you judge its strategies. Answer each question in 2–3 sentences with evidence.

| Idea (Session) | Question to answer |
|----------------|--------------------|
| **Two-sided network** (1.3) | Which groups does the platform connect? What does each group want from the other? |
| **Network effects** (1.2) | Does the platform become more useful as more users join? Is the effect direct (same group) or indirect (the other group)? |
| **Penguin effect** (1.2) | How did the company get its first users when nobody wanted to join first? |
| **Winner-takes-all** (1.4) | Is the market moving toward one winner? What is the company's market position, and can users easily use rival platforms at the same time (multihoming)? |

## Part B: Strategy Analysis (Modules 2 and 3)

Choose **2 or 3 strategies** from the menu below that the company actually used between 2021 and 2025. **At least one** of them must be a strategy that failed, partly failed, or carries a clear risk. Real companies make mistakes, and explaining a mistake shows real understanding.

### Strategy Menu

| Strategy (Session) | The rules you will check |
|--------------------|--------------------------|
| **Data Feedback Loop** (2.1) | 1. Start with the decision, not the data<br>2. Use strong signals, and check weak signals before you act<br>3. Close the feedback loop and measure one result<br>4. Earn trust: ask permission, collect only what you need, and give value back |
| **Asymmetric Pricing** (2.2) | 1. Subsidize the side that the other side comes for<br>2. Charge the side that gains the most from the match<br>3. Check price sensitivity before you set a price<br>4. Make sure the money side covers the subsidy, and protect the free side |
| **Up-Sell and Cross-Sell** (2.3) | 1. Price by the value the customer sees, not only by your cost<br>2. Build tiers for different customer groups (good, better, best)<br>3. Up-sell at the moment of need, and show the extra value<br>4. Cross-sell products that help the customer finish the same goal |
| **Platform Envelopment** (3.1) | 1. Enter a market where your users already are<br>2. Use your shared parts again<br>3. Make the new product good enough and easier to use<br>4. Let users choose, and play fair |
| **Cannibalization** (3.2) | 1. Compare with the future, not with today<br>2. Count the net gain, not only the new sales<br>3. Give the new product its own team and its own goals<br>4. Move customers step by step, and keep their trust |
| **Disruption** (3.3) | 1. Start where the leader is not looking<br>2. Make it simpler and cheaper, and good enough for the job<br>3. Choose a model the leader does not want to copy<br>4. Improve step by step, then move up to better customers |
| **Open Innovation** (3.4) | Use the questions from Session 3.4: Does the company open its platform to outside developers or partners (for example, through APIs)? What do partners gain? How does the company keep quality and control? |

### Four Steps for Each Strategy

Analyze every strategy you choose with the same four steps:

1. **What the company did**: Describe the move with the year and a source.
2. **Rule check**: For each rule of the strategy, decide whether the company **followed** it, **broke** it, or whether it is **unclear**, and give one piece of evidence. Use a table like this:

   | Rule | Followed / Broke / Unclear | Evidence |
   |------|----------------------------|----------|
   | Rule 1: ... | Followed | ... |
   | Rule 2: ... | Broke | ... |

3. **Result**: Show what happened, with at least one number from your performance evidence (before and after the move).
4. **Verdict**: Did the strategy work, fail, or is it too early to tell? Connect the verdict to the rule check.

:::{.callout-tip}
## Use the class tools
Reuse the tools and templates from each session (for example, the signal questions from Session 2.1, the subsidy calculation from Session 2.2, and the overlap rate and CAC comparison from Session 3.1). Your evidence will be stronger and your work will be faster.
:::

## Part C: Recommendation — "If I Ran This Company"

Imagine you are the owner of the company. Use the method from Session 2.4:

1. **Name the problem** the company faces today in one sentence, without a solution in it.
2. **Choose one strategy** from the menu to solve it, and show that its rules can be followed with evidence.
3. **Compare** it with one other option using the same numbers (expected result, time to result, main risk).
4. **Plan the next step**: which strategy should come after it, and why.

### What to Write in Each Section

**1. Company Introduction** (1 page)

- What does the company do, and when was it founded?
- Why did you choose this company?
- Which 2–3 strategies will you analyze, and why these?

**2. Platform Diagnosis** (1.5 pages)

- **Sides**: Which groups does the platform connect? What does each group want from the other?
- **Network effects**: Does the platform become more useful as more users join? Direct or indirect?
- **First users**: How did the company solve the penguin effect (nobody wants to join first)?
- **Market position**: Is the company the leader? Do users also use rival platforms?

**3. Strategy Analysis** (5 pages, about 2 pages per strategy)

Write one sub-section for each strategy, with the same four parts:

1. **What the company did**: one paragraph describing the move, with the year and a source.
2. **Rule check**: a table with the four rules of the strategy. For each rule, write *Followed*, *Broke*, or *Unclear*, plus one piece of evidence.
3. **Result**: numbers before and after the move (for example, users in 2021 and 2024). A simple chart is welcome.
4. **Verdict**: did it work, fail, or is it too early to tell? Explain which rules caused this result.

Remember: at least one strategy must have failed or carry a clear risk.

**4. Recommendation** (2.5 pages)

Write as if you are the owner of the company:

1. **Problem**: the biggest problem the company faces today, in one sentence. Do not include the solution.
2. **Strategy**: the strategy you choose to solve it, and why its rules can be followed (with evidence).
3. **Comparison**: one other option, compared with the same numbers (expected result, time to result, main risk).
4. **Next step**: which strategy should come after this one, and why.

**5. References**

- Every source in your report, in APA 7th edition format.

**6. Appendix: Evidence Table**

- One row for every factual claim in the report: *claim*, *evidence (number or fact)*, *source link*, *year*.

# Tips for Success

## Analysis Approach

1. **Start Early**: Choose your company early so Chapter 1 is ready in Week 10
2. **Collect Evidence First**: Gather 5 years of financial statements or performance indicators before starting analysis
3. **Create Timeline**: Map major strategic moves chronologically
4. **Evidence First**: Review performance trends before judging strategies
5. **Connect Strategies**: Look for how one strategy helped or hurt another (for example, free users from asymmetric pricing feeding a data feedback loop)
6. **Be Critical**: Include at least one strategy that failed or carries a clear risk

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Too descriptive**: Don't just describe what the company does - analyze WHY
- **Ignoring evidence**: Every strategic claim should link to a number or fact with a source in your evidence table
- **Strategy forcing**: Choose only strategies the company really used; do not force a strategy to fit
- **Lack of evolution**: Show CHANGE over 5 years, not just current state
- **No recommendations**: Don't just analyze - suggest improvements

## Writing Quality

- Use business-appropriate language
- Define technical terms
- Use active voice
- Include transition sentences
- Proofread carefully
- Use tables and figures effectively
