THE STRATEGY IN ONE PARAGRAPH

What is the Up-Sell and Cross-Sell Strategy?

Earn more from customers who already use a product by offering them more value, not by charging everyone more for the same thing. Up-selling offers a higher, better version of what the customer already uses. Cross-selling offers a related product that helps the customer finish the same goal. Both work only when the price matches the value the customer sees and the offer comes at the right moment.

Use it when many customers use a product but few pay, or when a company wants more revenue without simply raising prices for everyone.

WORDS YOU NEED FIRST

Up-sell and cross-sell

Up-sellOffering a higher tier or bigger version of the same product.
Example: a free user who runs out of storage is offered Plus.
Cross-sellOffering a different but related product.
Example: a Plus user is offered an exam note pack for $6.00.
SESSION 2.3 / KEY TERMS 2 OF 3SLIDE 5 / 30
WORDS YOU NEED FIRST

Two rates that measure the strategy

Conversion rate
(upgrade rate)The percentage of users who move to a paid tier.
Conversion rate = users who upgraded / users who could upgrade x 100
Example: 1,000 / 20,000 x 100 = 5%.
Attach rateThe percentage of customers who also buy the cross-sold product.
Attach rate = buyers of the add-on / customers who saw the offer x 100
Example: 30 of 200 Pro users buy the exam pack: 30 / 200 x 100 = 15%.

THE FOUR RULES / RULE 1

Rule 1: Price by the value the customer sees

Ask what problem each tier solves and how much that is worth to the customer, not only what it costs you.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE

Example

The exam planner saves a student about 3 hours a week before exams. That value explains why students pay $4.00 for Plus, even though storage costs StudyNest very little.

WHAT TO AVOID

Common mistake

Raising every price by the same amount because costs went up. Customers do not see more value, so many leave.

SESSION 2.3 / RULE 1 OF 4SLIDE 7 / 30
THE FOUR RULES / RULE 2

Rule 2: Build tiers for different customer groups

Good, better, best: a free tier to try, a middle tier for most paying customers, a top tier for heavy users.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE

Example

Free for students who take a few notes, Plus for students preparing for exams, Pro for study groups. The middle tier often sells best because it looks like good value next to the top tier.

WHAT TO AVOID

Common mistake

Tiers that are too similar, so customers cannot see why to pay more, or so many tiers that customers get confused.

SESSION 2.3 / RULE 2 OF 4SLIDE 8 / 30
THE FOUR RULES / RULE 3

Rule 3: Up-sell at the moment of need, and show the extra value

The best time to offer a higher tier is when the customer feels the limit of the current tier.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE

Example

When a free user's storage is full: "Your notes are full. Plus gives you 20 times more space and an exam planner for $4.00 a month."

WHAT TO AVOID

Common mistake

Showing upgrade pop-ups to every new user on the first day, before they know whether the app is useful.

SESSION 2.3 / RULE 3 OF 4SLIDE 9 / 30
THE FOUR RULES / RULE 4

Rule 4: Cross-sell what helps the customer finish the same goal

A good cross-sell feels like help, not like an extra sales push.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE

Example

In the two weeks before exams, StudyNest offers Plus and Pro users an exam note pack for $6.00. It fits what they are already trying to do.

WHAT TO AVOID

Common mistake

Offering unrelated products, such as phone cases in a study app, or pushing products customers do not need just to hit a sales target.

SESSION 2.3 / RULE 4 OF 4SLIDE 10 / 30
GOOD EXAMPLE 1

Dropbox storage plans

What happened

Dropbox gives a free Basic plan with 2 GB of storage. When users fill their space, Dropbox shows them how much more space the paid Plus plan gives.

Why it is good

  • The free tier lets people learn the product's value before paying. (Rule 2)
  • The offer appears exactly when the user feels the limit: the storage is full. (Rule 3)
  • The paid plan gives clearly more of what the user already values. (Rule 1)
SESSION 2.3 / GOOD EXAMPLE 1SLIDE 11 / 30
GOOD EXAMPLE 2

Amazon "Frequently bought together"

What happened

On a product page, Amazon shows items that customers often buy with that product, such as a memory card with a camera, and lets the shopper add them in one click.

Why it is good

  • The items help the customer use the main product, so the offer feels like help. (Rule 4)
  • The offer appears while the customer is already deciding to buy. (Rule 3)
  • The customer can easily ignore it, so it does not damage trust.
SESSION 2.3 / GOOD EXAMPLE 2SLIDE 12 / 30
BAD EXAMPLE 1

Netflix's 2011 price change

What happened

In July 2011, Netflix split its streaming plus DVD-by-mail plan into two plans. A customer who wanted both now paid $15.98 a month instead of $9.99, a 60% increase. Netflix lost about 800,000 U.S. subscribers in the next quarter, and its CEO later apologized.

Why it is bad

  • Customers paid much more for the same service, with no new value. (Rule 1)
  • It was a price increase for everyone, not an up-sell customers could choose.
  • It hit all customers at once, so many left instead of paying more.
SESSION 2.3 / BAD EXAMPLE 1SLIDE 13 / 30
BAD EXAMPLE 2

Wells Fargo's cross-selling targets

What happened

In 2016, U.S. regulators found that Wells Fargo employees had opened about 2 million bank and credit card accounts without customers' permission, under pressure to meet high cross-selling targets. The bank paid $185 million in fines.

Why it is bad

  • The products helped employees reach targets, not customers reach their goals. (Rule 4)
  • Customers did not choose the products, so it was not a real cross-sell.
  • Lost trust, fines, and lost customers cost far more than the extra sales.

BEFORE THE EXERCISES / TOOL 1

Tool 1: Revenue after a price change

When prices rise, some customers may leave. Count only the customers who stay.

USED IN EXERCISE 3

Formula

Revenue = customers who stay x new price

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE

Example: a gym

  • 100 members x $20.00 = $2,000 a month.
  • Price rises to $25.00 and 20 members leave.
  • 80 x $25.00 = $2,000: no extra money, and fewer members.
SESSION 2.3 / TOOL 1 OF 3SLIDE 16 / 30
BEFORE THE EXERCISES / TOOL 2

Tool 2: Revenue from up-selling and cross-selling

USED IN EXERCISES 2 AND 3

Two formulas

  • Up-sell: users who upgrade x price of the new tier
  • Cross-sell: customers who see the offer x attach rate x price of the add-on
WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE

Example: a music app

  • 5,000 free users; 3% upgrade to a $6.00 plan: 150 x $6.00 = $900 a month.
  • 1,000 paying users; 10% buy a $3.00 playlist pack: 100 x $3.00 = $300.
SESSION 2.3 / TOOL 2 OF 3SLIDE 17 / 30
BEFORE THE EXERCISES / TOOL 3

Tool 3: Trade-off and decision rule (from Session 2.1)

MEMO BULLET 2

Trade-off

What you give up to get something you value more.

MEMO BULLET 3

Decision rule

A promise, written before you start, about which result would make you change your decision.

Example: "If fewer than 2% of free users upgrade in 60 days, we will change the offer."

EXERCISE 3 / WORKED EXAMPLE

FitClub: raise the fee, or sell a training add-on?

To: Leo Kim, CEO, FitClub 1. Decision and evidence: We should sell a $15 personal-training add-on instead of raising every fee, because it matches what members want (Rule 4) and 10% of 1,500 members buying it adds 150 x $15 = $2,250 a month. 2. Biggest risk and trade-off: The weakest point is that members may not buy the add-on after the first month. We give up a fast, certain fee increase to keep members who would leave over a higher price. 3. Next step and decision rule: In the next 60 days we will offer the add-on to half of our members and measure the attach rate. We will change this decision if fewer than 5% of them buy it.
EXERCISE 3 / WORKED EXAMPLESLIDE 28 / 30
EXERCISE 3 / CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Raising all prices earns less; up-sell and cross-sell covers costs

The numbers

  • Option A: $7,540 if nobody leaves. If 25% leave: 750 x $5.20 + 150 x $11.70 = $5,655, less than today's $5,800.
  • Option B: 400 new Plus users x $4.00 = $1,600. 1,600 paying users x 20% = 320 buyers x $6.00 = $1,920.
  • Option B total: $5,800 + $1,600 + $1,920 = $9,320, above the $9,000 costs in exam season.

A strong memo

  • Usually chooses Option B.
  • Uses at least one number from Step 1.
  • Names a real risk: 2% is only an estimate, and pack sales come mainly in exam season.
  • States a clear decision rule.
