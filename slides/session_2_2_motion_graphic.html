ACT 04 / THE EXECUTIVE PLAYBOOK

The 4 Golden Rules of Asymmetric Pricing

Four non-negotiable principles every platform founder and corporate strategist must follow.

RULE 01

Subsidize the Side Others Come For

Identify the magnet group. If they leave, the entire platform collapses. Keep them free or heavily discounted.

CampusJobs: Keep Students Free
RULE 02

Charge the Side That Gains the Most

The money side either earns new revenue or eliminates costly friction. Charging them captures a fraction of their surplus.

CampusJobs: Charge Shops $25 / Post
RULE 03

Measure Price Sensitivity Before Pricing

Never guess. Run micro-surveys. A price that seems negligible to you ($2.00) can decimate your subsidy side's adoption.

Survey showed 60% student churn
RULE 04

Cover Costs & Protect the Free Side

The money side must pay enough to cover both sides' costs ($7,500 > $4,000). Purge spam and fake jobs to preserve trust.

Ban predatory recruiters
ACT 05 / INDUSTRY SHOWDOWN

Hall of Fame vs. Hall of Shame

How world-class companies won big—or crashed hard—based on these four rules.

🏆 The Masters (Followed the Rules)
Adobe Acrobat Reader RULE 01
Gave away Reader free to millions. Because everyone could open PDFs, enterprise creators gladly paid thousands for Acrobat Pro.
Google Search RULES 02 & 04
Billions search for free. Advertisers pay billions to capture targeted intent. Clear ad labels preserve user trust.
❌ The Disasters (Violated the Rules)
Unity Runtime Fee (2023) VIOLATED R1 & R3
Tried charging game developers per install. Alienated the creator side that brings players. Backlash forced the CEO to step down.
Groupon Merchant Deals VIOLATED RULE 02
Forced 50% discounts on local diners. Bargain shoppers never returned at full price. The money side lost money and fled.

Decision & Evidence (The Strategy)

We choose Option B: Keep students $0, charge shops $25 per job post. Following Rule 1 & Rule 2, subsidizing the student magnet preserves 6,000 active candidates, generating 30 apps/post and $7,500 monthly revenue, surpassing our $4,000 operating costs.

2

Weakest Condition & Key Trade-Off (The Risk)

The primary risk is that shops hesitate if hiring speed slows down. We intentionally sacrifice direct student fees to safeguard network liquidity and prevent the 60% student drop experienced under Option A.
